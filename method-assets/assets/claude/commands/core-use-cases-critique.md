# /core-use-cases-critique

> PM critique of the drafted **Core Use Cases** artifact — one design-rail CI job. Verdict only; the PM never rewrites the model.

**Arguments** — none. Kind, job mode, branch, and project come from the ambient `AIARCH_*` env.

**Agent + skills.** **Adopt the `product-manager` role**: read `.claude/agents/product-manager.md` and act per that charter yourself, in this session. Do NOT dispatch a subagent or use an Agent/Task tool — the CI job runs single-session; you ARE the product-manager. Judge against **[[the-method-core-use-cases]]** and [[the-method-project-state]] for the tool flow.

**Checks.** Call `run_checks()` until it reports every check passing, and finish with one full `run_checks()` call. Do not run the checks yourself.

## Steps

> **Operator notes first.** First call `getOperatorNotes`; if it returns notes, act on them before anything else. They are the operator's steer for this attempt (a dispatch note or a send-back) and outrank the defaults below. Judge the draft against the note too: do not send back the change the note directs, nor ask for what the note rules out of scope.

1. **Read the draft** with `getDraftSlot` and its committed predecessors with `getCommittedSlot`.
1a. **Read the coverage table** with `getUseCaseCoverage`. It is derived from the draft and stores nothing: one row per path the diagrams derive, with the columns **Use case · Path · Guards · Door · Outcomes the actor sees · Problems**. *Door* is the Client the actor's first act enters through, with its surface; *Outcomes the actor sees* are the `sendSignal` and `objectNode` labels on the path, verbatim; *Problems* are the derivation's `SCN-*` codes. Judge the customer-facing half — is each outcome the one the raw use case promises? — and leave the structural half to the rules.
    - **Outcomes.** A row whose *Outcomes the actor sees* reads **NONE** is a path that ends in nothing the actor is shown: that is a REVISE `[UC-PATH-OUTCOME]`, and the comment names the path id. Likewise a decision whose branches rejoin before one shared outcome ("shows X, or Y") is a REVISE `[UC-PATH-OUTCOME-DISTINCT]`: it asks that two paths a decision separates never end on the same outcome nodes, so each guarded branch gets its own `sendSignal` or `objectNode` (split a "shows X, or Y" signal into one per branch, drawn before the merge).
    - **Door, at this slot.** No Client exists before the architecture and no contract before construction, so at the core-use-cases review each row's Door reads **NONE**, and the Problems column carries `SCN-ACTOR-PATH-CLIENT` on every path an actor acts on. Both are expected here and neither is a ground to send the draft back. What slot 4 can show about the door is the actor's own act: every path passes through a node whose `linkedActorId` names a declared actor `[UC-ACTOR-DOOR]` (under "Actors and lanes" below). Which Client the actor enters by, and whether the answer comes back to it, is judged at the architecture (on an amendment after the System is committed the column may name a Client; it is still the architecture's review that answers for it).
2. **Read the ledger first** with `getReviewThread`. If you have critiqued before, critique the **delta** since your last verdict — not the artifact from scratch. Read `getCritique` to see your own prior verdict and notes — critique the delta against them.
3. **Apply verdict discipline** (anti-thrash — binding):
   - "revise" REQUIRES new, actionable comments tied to specific artifact content.
   - Never relitigate a resolved thread: if the architect responded to your comment, either accept the response or approve-with-noted-reservation. Repeating an already-answered comment is a defect.
   - Severity honesty: only defects against the mission/requirements justify "revise". Taste-level preferences are recorded as comments on an **approve**.
   - Co-discovery: you co-discover; if you object, say which raw use case is core and why — customer reality is your authority, abstraction taste is the architect's.
   - Core-use-cases doctrine you MUST enforce (ch. 4 §2.1: a core use case is an abstraction of the business essence, not a customer-listed feature):
     - **Count.** REVISE if there are fewer than 2 or more than 6 core use cases — more than 6 means the architect has not abstracted enough; exactly 1 may mean over-abstraction (look for distinct business pillars).
     - **Rejection trail.** REVISE if any nonCore use case lacks a rejection reason, or names a `variationOf` that is not the `id` of a core use case.
     - **Essence trail.** REVISE if any core decision's `essenceRationale` is missing or vacuous, or describes a feature rather than the essence of the business — the rationale must argue why the use case is shared by all customers, nearly immutable, and differentiating (an abstraction, not a feature restatement).
     - **No channel-shaped use case.** REVISE if any use case is really a delivery channel or client surface (the mobile/web/API/voice version of the same behavior) — channels are permutations of one behavior, never separate use cases.
     - **No CRUD core.** REVISE if any core use case is create/read/update/delete mechanics ("Create Order", "Update Order") — mechanics are never business essence.
     - **Volatility exercise.** REVISE if the flows never cross the committed `.volatilities` seams: each accepted volatility should be exercised by at least one core flow, or its absence explicitly justified. Flows that exercise no committed volatility are restating the customer's current manual process, not the product's required behavior.
     - **Abstraction, not restatement.** REVISE if the core list is the customer's own task names unchanged — real subsumption means several raw use cases collapse under a higher (often newly named) abstraction; verify that the cores actually subsume raw entries rather than relabel them one-for-one.
     - **Realizable actions.** REVISE if any action node only returns, renders, or refuses on a result an earlier node already produced; it must be a note. An action node is a place where some component makes a NEW call: the architecture realizes each action with one. A refusal the system records, notifies or audits is a real call and may stay an action. When you send a node back, the user-visible outcome must survive as the note — REVISE an amendment that deletes the behavior instead of reclassifying it.
     - **What the actor sees.** Every test of a surface is derived from these diagrams, so a path that ends in no outcome for the actor yields a test that asserts nothing. REVISE if an actor's act is drawn as a labelled start instead of an action node `[UC-START-NOT-ACTION]`; if a path carrying an actor's input has no `sendSignal` (what the actor is told) or `objectNode` (the state the actor sees) after it `[UC-PATH-OUTCOME]`; if two paths a decision separates end on the same outcome nodes, or one branch's outcomes are all shared by the other, so the test derived from them cannot tell the branches apart `[UC-PATH-OUTCOME-DISTINCT]`; or if a path in the coverage table starts with one actor's act and shows the outcome only to another actor (every outcome on it addressed to another declared actor; an outcome with no `linkedActorId` and no actor lane counts as the asker's), typically different actors' entries merged and then split by who asked, which the derivation cannot tell apart `[UC-OUTCOME-TO-ASKER]`; or if the outcome the raw use case promises the asking actor is drawn only as something told to someone else (another actor's lane, an announcement to all). These rules, with `UC-ACTOR-DOOR` and `UC-OUTCOME-TO-ASKER`, run when the draft is recorded; your part is to judge that each outcome is the one the customer's raw use case promises, not merely that one exists.
     - **Judged at the architecture, not here.** Where the actor enters and where the answer lands need the System's Clients and dynamic views, and the scenario rules need the contracts, so none of the following is a ground to send back the core use cases. When the architecture is recorded: only the surface half of `UC-OUTCOME-AT-ASKING-SURFACE` is judged there (the actor half is `UC-OUTCOME-TO-ASKER`, judged here): the outcome is shown on the Client the actor used to ask `[UC-OUTCOME-AT-ASKING-SURFACE]`; a branch an actor decides (a decision whose `decidedBy` is that actor) has a step in which that actor calls a Client `[CC-DECIDED-BY-ACTOR-STEP]`; every swim-lane `roleName` names a declared actor or a System component `[UC-LANE-RESOLVES]`. Once the contracts are recorded in construction: every input reaches a contracted component `[SCN-UNADDRESSED]`, every path with an actor's input reaches a contracted Client `[SCN-ACTOR-PATH-CLIENT]`, and every Client has a contract `[TP-CLIENT-CONTRACTED]`. A concern you foresee for them goes on the record as a comment on an approve, for the architect to carry into the architecture.
     - **Actors and lanes.** REVISE if any use case has no actors, or a lane meant for an actor's own acts names no declared actor of that use case (its `role` or `id`). Lanes for the system's work name components that do not exist yet, and are judged at the architecture (above). The rules above judge only nodes whose `linkedActorId` (or `decidedBy`) is a declared actor's id, so a diagram that links none judges nothing and looks clean. REVISE if a `clientAction` use case has no node linked to a declared actor, or any path passes through none `[UC-ACTOR-DOOR]` (the finding says "set linkedActorId to one of: <declared actor ids>"). REVISE too if a declared human actor of a `clientAction` use case has no node linked to it, or no `sendSignal` linked to it (nor `objectNode` in its lane) that says what it is told `[UC-ACTOR-UNSERVED]`: each declared human actor needs its own act and its own outcome, not only one of them. Do NOT revise for the two kinds asked less `[UC-ACTOR-UNSERVED]`: an agent actor (`kind: agent`, or a role such as "Team Agent") acts through its principal's act and owes only its outcome, and a `kind: system` actor owes nothing (a linked node for a system actor is refused once the architecture is recorded, so never ask for one `[UC-ACTOR-UNSERVED]`). A clean record is not evidence the actor is modelled: check that the nodes where an actor acts carry `linkedActorId`, the field's real name.
   - **Your revise is a floor, not a suggestion.** A "revise" on the core use cases holds the gate for a human under every review preset, including the one that otherwise approves unattended; the human can approve over it only as an override, with a recorded reason. Spend it on a path that shows the actor nothing or that no actor's act enters, not on taste.
4. **Record the verdict** with `setCritiqueVerdict` (approve/revise + comments). You MUST record your verdict with `setCritiqueVerdict` before finishing — a critique job that ends without recording a verdict fails the pipeline.
5. **Finish** with `publishDraft` (exactly once). You have no `putDraftModel`; do not attempt to fix the model yourself.
